1 - Indian cobra
Common name: Indian cobra
Scientific name: Naja naja
Venom status: Venomous (primarily neurotoxic, some cardiotoxic/ cytotoxic effects)
Typical habitat: Agricultural fields, villages, forest edges, scrub, near human settlements; often found where rodents are abundant.
Activity pattern: Mainly nocturnal/crepuscular, but can be active by day.
Key traits: Distinctive hood; many individuals have a “spectacle” pattern on the back of the hood; smooth scales; color highly variable (brown, yellowish, black, even lighter morphs).
Distribution: Widespread across the Indian subcontinent (India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka) with regional variation in color and pattern.
Notes: One of the “Big Four” medically important Indian snakes; significant cause of serious snakebite. Often worshipped in Indian culture.

2 - Common krait
Common name: Common krait
Scientific name: Bungarus caeruleus
Venom status: Venomous (strongly neurotoxic)
Typical habitat: Rural and peri-urban areas, fields, near houses, termite mounds, rodent burrows; often close to human habitation.
Activity pattern: Nocturnal; many bites occur at night, sometimes on sleeping individuals.
Key traits: Glossy dark (blue-black) body with narrow white crossbands; bands can be faint in adults; round tail; relatively small head; smooth scales.
Distribution: Widely distributed across much of India and neighboring regions in the subcontinent, especially plains and lowlands.
Notes: Frequently causes severe envenoming with minimal local swelling; respiratory paralysis is a major danger. Part of the “Big Four.”

3 - Russell’s viper
Common name: Russell’s viper
Scientific name: Daboia russelii
Venom status: Venomous (haemotoxic, nephrotoxic, cardiotoxic effects)
Typical habitat: Open fields, scrubland, plantations, farmlands, rural outskirts; often found near villages.
Activity pattern: Mostly nocturnal but can be seen by day; often basks or rests in cover.
Key traits: Heavy-bodied viper; series of large dark oval or circular spots with lighter centers running along the dorsum; strongly triangular head; loud hiss when disturbed.
Distribution: Widely distributed in India (excluding some far northeastern/higher Himalayan areas) and across parts of South Asia.
Notes: Another “Big Four” species; responsible for many severe bites with bleeding, shock, and kidney injury.

4 - Indo-Chinese rat snake
Common name: Indo-Chinese rat snake (often called Chinese/Indochinese rat snake)
Scientific name: Ptyas korros
Venom status: Non-venomous
Typical habitat: Fields, secondary forests, gardens, near human habitation in parts of Southeast Asia; in India, more common in northeastern regions.
Activity pattern: Diurnal; active and fast-moving.
Key traits: Slender, long-bodied snake; brown to olive with lighter underside; somewhat similar overall build to Ptyas mucosa but distribution and markings differ.
Distribution: Primarily Southeast Asia (Thailand, Myanmar, Laos, Cambodia, Vietnam, southern China), with occurrence in parts of Northeast India.
Notes: Non-venomous rodent-eater; can be confused with other rat snakes; important if your dataset includes Northeast Indian records.

5 - King cobra
Common name: King cobra
Scientific name: Ophiophagus hannah
Venom status: Venomous (neurotoxic)
Typical habitat: Dense forests, bamboo thickets, riverine forests, plantations in Western Ghats and Northeast India; often near water.
Activity pattern: Mainly diurnal.
Key traits: World’s longest venomous snake; long, slender body relative to size; narrow hood (less wide than Naja naja); olive to greenish color with lighter bands in juveniles; distinct head shape.
Distribution: Patchy across South and Southeast Asia: in India mainly Western Ghats and Northeast (Assam region and neighboring areas), extending into Southeast Asia.
Notes: Predominantly feeds on other snakes; fewer human bites than Big Four but bites are serious; often a flagship species for conservation.

6 - Banded krait
Common name: Banded krait
Scientific name: Bungarus fasciatus
Venom status: Venomous (neurotoxic)
Typical habitat: Wet fields, marshy areas, near rivers, forest edges; often near water bodies in eastern/northeastern India and Southeast Asia.
Activity pattern: Nocturnal; hides in burrows or vegetation by day.
Key traits: Thick-bodied snake with broad alternating black and yellow bands; triangular body cross-section; head often dark; tail blunt.
Distribution: Northeast and eastern India, Bangladesh, and broad parts of Southeast Asia.
Notes: Medically significant where present; can be confused with non-venomous banded snakes; often less aggressive than some other kraits but still dangerous.

7 - Ornate flying snake
Common name: Ornate flying snake
Scientific name: Chrysopelea ornata
Venom status: Mildly venomous rear-fanged (not typically dangerous to humans)
Typical habitat: Forests, wooded regions, plantations, gardens; strongly arboreal.
Activity pattern: Diurnal; active in trees.
Key traits: Slender, brightly patterned snake; intricate network of greenish/yellow and black markings; known for “gliding” by flattening body and launching from trees.
Distribution: India (especially Western Ghats and parts of peninsula), Sri Lanka, and Southeast Asia.
Notes: Famous for its gliding behavior; occasionally misidentified due to striking pattern but medical significance is low compared to vipers and cobras.

8 - Indian rat snake
Common name: Indian rat snake / Oriental rat snake
Scientific name: Ptyas mucosa
Venom status: Non-venomous
Typical habitat: Fields, villages, urban areas, forests; highly tolerant of human-modified environments.
Activity pattern: Diurnal; often seen moving rapidly in open areas.
Key traits: Long, slender, fast snake; brown, olive, or yellowish body; round pupil; head distinct but not viper-like; may flatten neck slightly when threatened (confused with cobras).
Distribution: Widespread across India and much of South Asia; one of the most common large snakes near humans.
Notes: Harmless but often killed due to fear; major controller of rodent populations.

9 - Checkered keelback / Asiatic water snake
Common name: Checkered keelback, Asiatic water snake
Scientific name: Xenochrophis piscator (often treated as Fowlea piscator)
Venom status: Non-venomous
Typical habitat: Ponds, lakes, canals, rice fields, wetlands; semi-aquatic.
Activity pattern: Primarily diurnal; often seen basking or swimming.
Key traits: Distinct checkered pattern of dark and light scales along the body; keeled scales give a rough texture; moderately robust body for a non-venomous snake.
Distribution: Widely distributed across much of India and neighboring countries in suitable wetland habitats.
Notes: Frequently mistaken for venomous snakes due to habitat and body shape; commonly encountered around water.

10 - Buff striped keelback / Striped keelback
Common name: Buff striped keelback, Striped keelback
Scientific name: Amphiesma stolatum (now often Hebius stolatum)
Venom status: Non-venomous
Typical habitat: Grasslands, agricultural fields, edges of wetlands, roadside vegetation.
Activity pattern: Diurnal; small, active snake.
Key traits: Brown to olive body with one or more distinct buff or yellowish stripes along the sides or dorsum; keeled scales; slender body.
Distribution: Widespread in many parts of India and South Asia.
Notes: Common small snake; sometimes confused with juvenile venomous snakes due to stripes and habitat; harmless.

11- Painted bronzeback
Common name: Painted bronzeback (a bronzeback tree snake)
Scientific name: Dendrelaphis pictus
Venom status: Non-venomous
Typical habitat: Trees, shrubs, forest edges, gardens; strongly arboreal.
Activity pattern: Diurnal; highly active, often moving through branches.
Key traits: Slender arboreal snake; striking coloration with bronze/green back and contrasting lateral stripes; large eyes adapted to arboreal life.
Distribution: Primarily Southeast Asia; in India largely limited to parts of the Northeast and coastal/forest areas depending on local records.
Notes: Similar ecological niche to Dendrelaphis tristis; harmless but very fast; visually distinctive for your dataset.

12 - Asian vine snake / Green vine snake
Common name: Asian vine snake / Green vine snake
Scientific name: Ahaetulla prasina
Venom status: Mildly venomous rear-fanged (generally not dangerous to humans)
Typical habitat: Forests, plantations, gardens; arboreal, often in shrubs and low trees.
Activity pattern: Diurnal.
Key traits: Extremely slender, elongated head and body; bright green coloration with pointed snout; characteristic “vine-like” appearance; excellent camouflage among foliage.
Distribution: Southeast Asia; in India, related vine snakes occur, but A. prasina specifically is more Southeast Asian—check local records if using Indian data.
Notes: Classic vine snake appearance; may be confused with other Ahaetulla species—taxonomy and locality matter for labels.

13 - Mock viper
Common name: Mock viper
Scientific name: Psammodynastes pulverulentus
Venom status: Mildly venomous rear-fanged (generally low risk to humans)
Typical habitat: Forest floor, leaf litter, bushes; often in moist habitats.
Activity pattern: Mostly nocturnal/crepuscular.
Key traits: Small snake that visually mimics true vipers; stoutish body, distinct head, pattern somewhat reminiscent of pit vipers; eyes and head shape differ on close inspection.
Distribution: Southeast Asia, some records extending toward Northeast India; more common outside mainland India but important for regional datasets.
Notes: Important “lookalike” species for your model; can be mistakenly identified as a viper by non-experts.

14 - White-lipped pit viper
Common name: White-lipped pit viper
Scientific name: Trimeresurus albolabris
Venom status: Venomous (local tissue damage, haemotoxic/coagulopathic effects)
Typical habitat: Moist forests, plantation edges, bushes; often in low vegetation.
Activity pattern: Mostly nocturnal.
Key traits: Green pit viper with lighter belly; characteristic pale/white lips or labial scales; triangular head with heat-sensing pits; prehensile tail.
Distribution: Southeast Asia; in India mainly recorded from Northeast (Assam, surrounding areas).
Notes: Medically important where present; classic green pit viper look; important to differentiate from other Trimeresurus species.

15 - Mangrove pit viper / Purple-lipped pit viper
Common name: Mangrove pit viper / Purple-lipped pit viper
Scientific name: Trimeresurus purpureomaculatus
Venom status: Venomous (local tissue damage, haemotoxic/coagulopathic effects)
Typical habitat: Mangroves, coastal forests, lowland swampy areas.
Activity pattern: Mostly nocturnal.
Key traits: Pit viper with distinct coloration; often dark body with purplish or reddish markings; triangular head, heat-sensing pits.
Distribution: Coastal Southeast Asia; some records in the Andaman/ Nicobar region and nearby coasts; check local data if focusing narrowly on mainland India.
Notes: Important pit viper species in mangrove habitats; visually striking; may appear in Indian coastal observation datasets.
